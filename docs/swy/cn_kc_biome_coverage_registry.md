# CN/Kc coverage registry

Started 2026-09-22. Tracks, per biome (and separately, per climate zone × crop type for agricultural land), whether a real, defensible source exists for curve number (CN) and crop coefficient (Kc) beyond GCN250/Kamble's global generic defaults — and what it would take to close the gap where one doesn't exist yet. Land-cover type isn't the registry's own organizing unit; it's what gets routed into one of the buckets below, each of which has its own real key. This is the artifact the SWY biome-correction architecture consults when building per-pixel CN_A-D/Kc_1-12 rasters, fed to `inspring` via its existing raster-override mechanism instead of a single flat lookup table.

## The routing logic: three buckets, not one scheme

The question for any land-cover type is which of three things actually determines its hydrology:

1.  **Natural vegetation and climate** — forest (all types), grassland, shrubland, wetland/marsh, mangrove, bare/sparse ground. Keyed by **WWF terrestrial biome** (the same 16-class scheme already used elsewhere in this project — `data/vector_basedata/Biome.gpkg`), since biome encodes exactly the climate-and-vegetation-structure variation that drives CN and Kc for these types. This is the main table below.
2.  **Human management practice** — cropland. Biome can't capture this: it describes the *underlying* vegetation a location would have, not what someone actually planted and how they farm it. Needs its own key, **Köppen climate zone × crop type**, tracked separately below.
3.  **Physically near-universal, no correction needed** — water (Inland Water, Fishpond: fixed CN=100, Kc≈1.05) and, as a lower-confidence but reasonable simplification, built-up/urban (fixed CN, fixed Kc≈0.10) — pavement/drainage hydrology is far less climate-dependent than vegetation-mediated infiltration, though this hasn't been specifically checked against literature the way the other two buckets have.

This is meant to be built incrementally, not completed before it's useful. Every row starts at "not yet checked" and gets real content as literature is found — there's no dependency between rows, and a mostly-empty registry with a few well-sourced cells is already more honest and more useful than one undifferentiated global default.

## Status tiers

**Terms:**

- **Validated**: a real study measured CN/Kc in this biome or a documented close analog.

- **Analog**: no exact-biome study, but a defensible ecological stand-in exists, flagged as a substitution, not a direct measurement.

- **Partial**:real evidence in hand, real gaps remain.

- **Unresolved**: searched, nothing usable found — possibly a structural limitation of the method itself, not just a literature gap.

- **Model breaks down**: a real study *tested* the standard method here and found the framework itself doesn't fit the physical process — stronger than "unresolved," and comes with a tested alternative

- **Not yet checked**.

## Bucket 2 — cropland (Köppen climate zone × crop type, separate from biome)

Climate layer **sourced**: Beck et al. (2018), 1km global Köppen-Geiger, 30 classes, in `data/swy/shared/koppen_geiger/`. Crop-type layer **sourced** (2026-09-23): MapSPAM2020 v2.2 dominant-crop-by-area (`06b_fetch_mapspam_dominant_crop.py`) — Philippine Annual/Perennial Crop is rice (50.6%), coconut (16.9%), maize (15.5%). Crop-calendar layer **sourced** (2026-09-23): Sacks et al. (2010) SAGE global planting/harvest dates, filled 5-arcmin netCDF, fetched and clipped to the Philippines AOI (`06c_fetch_sacks_crop_calendar.py`) — rice main-season plant DOY~130/harvest DOY~301 (171-day cycle), maize plant DOY~130/harvest DOY~225 (95-day cycle); no second-season signal for either crop in this AOI per Sacks' own data (a real dataset limitation, not a bug). Coconut has no calendar in Sacks (expected — perennial tree crop, falls under Perennial Crop not Annual Crop, doesn't block the Annual Crop fix). **Not yet done**: building the actual per-pixel cropland Kc rasters from these two layers + FAO-56 stage tables (`kc_calculator.xlsx`), and wiring them into a test run the same way 07d/07e did for forest/grassland.

**Status 2026-09-25:** Köppen layer on disk but not used by any script yet; the Philippines (Af/Am only) can't exercise a climate key. The Philippines comparison showed cropland CN is the main error source (Annual Crop B 2.28x vs her-params reference; her CN alone brings it to 1.25x), so this bucket is where a global run's targeted corrections go. MapSPAM's irrigated/rainfed split (only the "ALL" layers are downloaded so far) is the third key needed: paddy and irrigated cropland behave differently from rainfed.

Known so far: Kamble et al. (2013) is validated for temperate, semi-arid-to-subhumid, dryland row-crop agriculture (US High Plains) — real coverage wherever that landscape type recurs (North China Plain, Ukrainian/Russian steppe, the Argentine Pampas, the Australian wheat belt), not a US-specific result once the crop-type layer lets it be applied by climate analog rather than geography. It does not cover tropical smallholder agriculture or paddy rice, which are structurally unlike its calibration domain (paddy in particular behaves more like a seasonal wetland than a dryland crop for parts of its cycle).

## Bucket 3 — physically near-universal, no correction needed

Water (Inland Water, Fishpond): fixed CN=100, Kc≈1.05, already implemented, not needing this registry. Built-up/urban: fixed CN and Kc≈0.10, already implemented — a reasonable simplification given impervious-surface hydrology is far less climate-dependent than vegetation-mediated infiltration, though this specific assumption hasn't been checked against literature the way the other buckets have (rainfall-intensity differences between tropical and temperate storms could matter more than assumed here).

**Combined forest+grassland/shrub test, 2026-09-23** (`07f_build_kc_combined_forest_grassland.py`, `09k_run_swy_ph_combined_forest_grassland_kc.py`, `18_combined_forest_grassland_comparison.py`): the two independently-validated Kc corrections were merged into one raster set and run together, confirming they stack cleanly without interacting. Forest's real improvement held exactly (Closed Forest B ratio 1.69→1.30, Open Forest 1.78→1.46); grassland/shrub's weak/mixed effect also held (Brush/Shrubs 0.703→0.725, Grassland 0.669→0.651). AOI-wide B aggregate barely moved (1.48→1.37, r 0.39→0.39) because Annual Crop's untouched 5.82× distortion (43.7% of the AOI) continues to dominate the aggregate — confirms cropland, not a forest/grassland refinement, is the remaining high-leverage gap.

**NDVI per-pixel Kc test, 2026-09-24** (`07g_build_kc_ndvi_perpixel.py`, `09l_run_swy_ph_ndvi_perpixel_kc.py`, `19_ndvi_perpixel_comparison.py`): fixes the Kamble-on-EVI calibration error (Kamble's NDVI-fit coefficients had been applied to EVI, which runs ~65% of NDVI here) by applying Kamble per pixel to NDVI for the five classes still on it (Annual Crop, Perennial Crop, Mangrove, Marshland/Swamp, Open/Barren). Forest and grassland/shrub Kc unchanged from 09k, so 09k→09l isolates the fix. QF identical (max diff 0, as expected). AOI-wide B ratio 1.37→1.10, r 0.39→0.56. Perennial Crop 1.67→0.91 (now close to 1:1 with visible within-class spread instead of a narrow band), Annual Crop 5.84→3.91, Mangrove 2.77→1.67. Closed Forest 1.30→1.30 and Open Forest 1.46→1.43: the forest result holds with cropland corrected. Two classes moved away from the baseline: Marshland/Swamp 0.99→0.46 and Open/Barren 0.59→0.41 (both small; Open/Barren has no fixed Kc and picks up vegetation from mixed 1km pixels). Annual Crop's remaining gap is probably CN, not Kc: our annual-mean Kc (0.81) is now close to WWF-SIPA's (0.91), but GCN250 cropland CN (57/68/75/78) sits ~10 points below theirs (67/78/85/89), matching our Annual Crop QF at 0.55× theirs.
