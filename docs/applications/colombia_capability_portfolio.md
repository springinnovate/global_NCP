## The repositioning thesis

The pipeline (hotspots, critical natural assets, exposure) is real, global-scale, reproducible
work — but by itself it reads as "spatial analyst." The stronger, more accurate positioning is:
**someone who sits at the join between biophysical evidence and the political economy of why NbS
implementation actually succeeds or fails on the ground.** Few people pair both halves personally —
that's the differentiator, not the maps alone.

## Two capability layers, and why the combination is the pitch

**Layer 1 — technical/biophysical (already built, already demonstrated):**

- Global, reproducible ES change pipeline: 8 InVEST services (9 with SWY in progress),
  1992–2020 realized change, 300m native resolution aggregated to 1.3M 10km cells.
- Three decision-ready outputs: WHERE decline concentrates, WHO is exposed, WHY (land-cover driver
  signal).
- Critical natural assets layer (opportunity, not just risk) from Chaplin-Kramer et al. 2022.
- Architecturally rerunnable at country scale, finer resolution, more services — a platform, not a
  one-off map. Already proven at country scale in Colombia (see below).

**Layer 2 — land systems science / political ecology / SES (the actual gap most technical rooms
have):**

- BA Economics, BSc Agricultural Economics, PhD work in land systems science and political
  ecology; working knowledge of socioecological systems (SES) framing.
- Direct research experience on commodity-driven agricultural frontier expansion — oil palm in the
  Colombian Llanos (Orinoquía) — a live, first-hand case of infrastructure and commodity
  incentives reshaping land systems faster than formal planning frameworks anticipate.
- A specific, non-obvious critique to offer rooms designing enabling frameworks (mitigation
  hierarchy, land-use planning, procurement): these are usually designed by people who model
  ecosystems or model finance, not by people who've studied how land users actually respond to
  formal rules — land tenure informality, elite capture, market-driven land conversion that routes
  around zoning on paper.

**The combination**: not "I make hotspot maps" but "I can generate the biophysical evidence *and*
tell you where an enabling framework built on paper-only assumptions will break in practice,
because I've studied a frontier where that already happened."

## Colombia / Llanos — the concrete offer, now with a track record

Colombia used to exist only inside the LAC regional aggregate. That's no longer true — a real,
country-level cut now exists (`docs/reports/colombia_clec/colombia_clec_report.qmd`, Spanish and
English, HTML with basic interactivity; a Spanish revealjs deck; 5 Colombia-specific analysis
scripts), confirmed to rerun cleanly on the same standardized global methodology. Not yet
generalized into a reusable "country-report toolbox" for other countries — a real next step, not
started.

This grounds the pitch in something demonstrated, not hypothetical: *"Here's a Colombia-national
hotspot + critical-asset cut — Llanos/Orinoquía agricultural frontier, Amazon transition zone,
páramo water towers feeding Bogotá — on the same methodology already shown globally, read through
a frontier-dynamics lens I have first-hand research grounding in."* Concrete result already found:
Colombia holds 2.93× its expected share of global critical natural assets relative to its land
area, and 57.9% of Colombia's change hotspots also fall inside a critical natural asset — a real
"avoid this first" signal, not a generic claim.

**Real, ongoing engagement, not a cold pitch**: this analysis is what led to direct contact with
WWF Colombia's science team (César Suárez and colleagues), including concrete, named collaboration
openings — Bogotá land-cover mapping methodology, a Smurfit land-cover accuracy-verification
project (a direct match to the LC_orinoquia classification-accuracy background), and an ongoing
IUCN Nature-based Solution qualification discussion. Full detail:
`project-capability-portfolio-pitch` memory.
