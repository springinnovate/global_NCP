Averaged across all evaluated cells, the five services move in different directions at different magnitudes: nitrogen export rose 2.2%, sediment export rose 3.6%, coastal risk was effectively flat (+0.04%, consistent with its inherently narrow coastal footprint), pollination rose 0.5%, and nature access fell 3.0%. Three of these five global averages — export volumes and nature access — point the same direction the hotspot analysis already identifies as adverse; the fourth, coastal risk, is simply too spatially confined for a global mean to be informative on its own. Pollination is the one genuine exception, and deliberately so: because InVEST models it as a function of land-cover class and neighborhood composition alone, a *rise* in modeled pollination can reflect cropland abandonment reverting to natural cover rather than any improvement in pollinator condition — the average masks this directly, a point we return to in the Discussion.

The global average, however, is the least interesting number here — what it obscures is where. Sediment export's 3.6% global rise concentrates overwhelmingly in the Tropical & Subtropical Moist Broadleaf Forest biome (+16.1%, more than four times the global figure) and, regionally, in Latin America & Caribbean (+15.6%, again roughly four times the global mean); the same region also shows the sharpest nitrogen export increase (+4.2% against a 2.2% global mean). Nature access shows a comparable concentration on the decline side: Flooded Grasslands & Savannas lose the most (−9.8% against a −3.0% global mean), followed by the same Tropical Moist Broadleaf biome that dominates the sediment signal (−8.3%). Notably, Flooded Grasslands & Savannas is simultaneously the biome with the *largest pollination gain* (+7.5%) — the same landscape registering the century's steepest loss of natural-habitat proximity is, by the model's own accounting, becoming more pollinator-suitable, which is exactly the kind of land-cover-mechanical artifact described above rather than a real ecological improvement.

Regional patterns diverge in ways a global average cannot show. Latin America & Caribbean is the clearest case: it leads every service in the adverse direction (sediment, nitrogen, nature access) except pollination, where it shows the steepest *decline* of any region (−5.5%) even as pollination rises almost everywhere else — the region driving the most volumetric damage elsewhere is also the one place where the pollination signal is least likely to be a land-abandonment artifact and most likely to reflect an actual loss. Income groups tell a related but distinct story, and one the hotspot-intensity analysis below depends on to be legible: lower-middle-income countries carry both the highest nitrogen export increase (+3.1%) and the steepest nature access decline (−5.0%) of any income group, while sediment export moves in opposite directions across the income gradient entirely — falling in low-income countries (−0.6%) while rising sharply in upper-middle-income ones (+5.4%). This is the same income group the hotspot analysis identifies as bearing the highest compound risk intensity; stating the underlying change pattern here, rather than only the resulting hotspot count, is what makes that later finding legible rather than asserted.

More generally, this three-level breakdown — global average, then biome, then region and income group — is not specific to these five services or this study period. Any directional change measured at fine spatial resolution can be characterized the same way: a single global mean establishes whether a service is moving, and nested breakdowns by ecological and socioeconomic grouping establish whether that movement is broadly distributed or concentrated enough to demand a geographically or demographically targeted response. The five services analyzed here happen to show every version of that pattern — export volumes concentrated in one biome and region, nature access concentrated in a different one, and pollination inverted between the two — which is itself the point: a single global number would have flattened all three into one unremarkable average.
