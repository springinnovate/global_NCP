## The repositioning thesis

The pipeline (hotspots, critical natural assets, exposure) is real, global-scale, reproducible
work — but by itself it reads as "spatial analyst." The stronger, more accurate positioning is:
**someone who sits at the join between biophysical evidence and the political economy of why
NbS implementation actually succeeds or fails on the ground.** That join is exactly what this
workshop series is structured around — Workshop 1 (valuation/spatial tools) and Workshop 3
(enabling frameworks/institutions) are usually staffed by two different kinds of people. Few
people in the room can speak credibly to both ends personally. That's the differentiator, not
the maps alone.

## Two capability layers, and why the combination is the pitch

**Layer 1 — technical/biophysical (already built, already demonstrated Monday):**
- Global, reproducible ES change pipeline: 8 InVEST services (9 with SWY in progress), 1992–2020
  realized change, 300m native resolution aggregated to 1.3M 10km cells.
- Three decision-ready outputs: WHERE decline concentrates, WHO is exposed, WHY (land-cover
  driver signal).
- Critical natural assets layer (opportunity, not just risk) from Chaplin-Kramer et al. 2022.
- Architecturally rerunnable at country scale, finer resolution, more services — a platform, not
  a one-off map.

**Layer 2 — land systems science / political ecology / SES (not yet visible to this audience at
all, and the actual gap in the room):**
- BA Economics, BSc Agricultural Economics, PhD work in land systems science and political
  ecology; working knowledge of socioecological systems (SES) framing.
- Direct research experience on commodity-driven agricultural frontier expansion — oil palm in
  the Colombian Llanos (Orinoquía) — a live, textbook case of exactly the tension this workshop
  series is trying to design around: infrastructure and commodity incentives reshaping land
  systems faster than formal planning frameworks anticipate.
- Political ecology gives a specific, non-obvious critique to offer Workshop 3's content
  (mitigation hierarchy, land-use planning, procurement): these frameworks are usually designed
  by people who model ecosystems or model finance, not by people who've studied how land users
  actually respond to formal rules — land tenure informality, elite capture, market-driven land
  conversion that routes around zoning on paper. That's a credible, first-hand-grounded point
  almost nobody else in that room can make.

**The combination**: not "I make hotspot maps" but "I can generate the biophysical evidence
*and* tell you where an enabling framework built on paper-only assumptions will break in
practice, because I've studied a frontier where that already happened."
